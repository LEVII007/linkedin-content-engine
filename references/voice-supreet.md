# Voice Profile — Supreet Deshpande

Built by reading his LinkedIn profile and 18 of his own posts (Sept 2026 back to ~6 months),
plus his comment replies. Every pattern below has a quoted example from a real post. Nothing here
is invented or inferred from adjectives.

Source: `linkedin.com/in/supreetdeshpande` · ~6,600 followers · profile read 2026-09-13.

## 3. Structure he actually uses

**A. Setup → reversal.** His single strongest and most repeated device. Two short clauses, contrast, then unpack.

> "The advice we got: 'pharma moves too slow for a startup.' The reality: 5 Top 10 pharma companies in 12 months."

> "I thought I understood what it meant to build and transform companies... Then I started Synthio Labs. And I realized there's a big difference between having the answer and owning the outcome."

**B. Outside-in analogy → the industry point.** The Weekend Pondering shape. He borrows a frame from
physics, film, or another founder, explains it properly, then maps it.

> "Q = mL... You keep adding energy, but the thermometer doesn't change... That's enterprise AI right now."

He does the teaching honestly — the full formula, the actual mechanism — before the mapping. The
analogy carries argument, not decoration.

**C. Scene → claim.** Opens with a concrete moment, then the point.

> "A year ago today, Sahitya walked into YC Alumni Demo Day in a white coat. Full physician attire. In front of 500+ founders and investors."

**D. The two-column judgment.** A test the reader can apply, marked with ❗ and ✅.

> "❗They're selling you the same platform you've had for a decade... ✅ They come to you and say: 'Everything we sold you for the past decade is now deprecated.'"

**E. The micro post.** Two to four lines, one thought, no build-up. Used for a podcast quote, a
conference, a single observation.

## 4. Sentence-level patterns

- **Fragments for emphasis, in pairs.** "Not incrementally. Structurally." / "You own what works. You own what doesn't."
- **Colon to set up the payoff.** "Here's the thing:" / "The mistake?" / "The question worth asking isn't..."
  These short discourse markers may recur. They are cadence, not sentences to copy; the words that
  follow them must be original to the current material.
- **Second person for the buyer's experience**, first person plural for Synthio. "You'll rarely have perfect information." / "We stopped trying to convince people..."
- **Numbers inline, in prose**, not on their own line. "5 of the top 10 global pharma companies are already building with us."
- **Names people and thanks them by name**, often a long tagged list on milestone posts.
- **Admits the hard part** without undercutting the claim. "The deals that almost fell through. The moments where the path wasn't clear."
- **Rhetorical question, then answer it himself.** He never leaves the question hanging for comments.

Length: two registers only. Micro (~200–400 characters) or essay (~1,500–2,600 characters). Almost
nothing in between.

## 5. Surface habits

- **Emoji: deliberate in essays, optional in micro posts.** Essays usually use 1–6, either as
  line-start bullets (🗣️ ⏱️ 🚀 ❗ ✅ →) or as a beat at the end of a thought. 🚀 is a common
  sign-off. Micro posts often use none. Emoji sprinkled mid-sentence do not read as him.
- **Hashtags: usually 4–6 at the end of essays; often none on micro posts.** Recurring set:
  `#Pharma` `#LifeSciences` `#AI` `#AgenticAI` `#VoiceAI` `#PharmaCommercial` `#SynthioLabs`
  `#EnterpriseAI` `#FounderJourney`.
- **Arrows `→` for example queries or lists.** "→ Which HCPs at Cleveland Clinic are actively involved in SCLC trials..."
- **Em dashes and short parentheticals** are frequent and normal for him.
- **Closing move** is one of three: an invitation ("drop me a note", "Would love your take if you're
  in the room for these vendor conversations", "Send us 5 NPIs"), a short punch line ("The white coat
  had a point.", "Agency > Pedigree (Period)"), or gratitude plus 🚀.
- **Comment replies are very short and warm.** "Thanks Sewa! Couldn't agree more" / "Shribhagwan cool
  stuff!" / "Congratulations Patrick, and a beautiful post! 🎉" Never a paragraph.

## 7. Never

Observed absences, not guesses. None of these appear in six months of posts.

- No politics, no memes, no off-topic personal content.
- No engagement bait: no "thoughts?", no polls, no "comment below", no "follow for more".
- No numbered listicles ("5 lessons I learned").
- No naming a customer or a deal that is not already public with their consent.
- No clinical claims about a drug, and no patient data.
- No criticising a named competitor. He criticises a *pattern* ("selling you the same platform for a decade"), never a company.
- No long comment threads — he replies in under ten words or not at all.
- No posting about internal engineering detail. Synthio Labs the company account does that; he does not.

## 8. Draft checklist

A draft is in his voice only if all applicable checks are true. The micro example in
`skills/linkedin-draft/examples.md` wins over a general surface-habit rule when they conflict.

1. Does it come from something he actually said, did, or decided? If the source is a news headline, stop.
2. For an essay, is there a reversal, an analogy, or a scene in the first three lines? For a micro
   post, is the complete thought clear without build-up?
3. Does it land one idea, not three?
4. Are the numbers ones he has already published, or newly evidenced?
5. Does an essay have a closing invitation, punch line, or thanks, with deliberate emoji where
   natural? A micro post may end directly and use no emoji.
6. Does an essay have 4–6 relevant hashtags? A micro post may have none.
7. Would a VP at a Top 10 pharma read this and think "he has been in my building"?

## 9. Conflicts with the generic rules in this repo

`references/voice.md` and `references/post-shapes.md` were written for an engineer publishing
technical findings. Several of their rules are wrong for Supreet, and **this file wins** where they
disagree:

| Generic rule | Reality for Supreet |
|---|---|
| "No emoji" implied; emoji listed as a thing to check for | Essays usually use deliberate emoji; micro posts may use none |
| "Hashtags: at most two, usually zero" | Essays usually use 4–6; micro posts may use none |
| "Numbers get their own line" | He writes numbers inline, in prose |
| "No engagement bait" | Correct in spirit — but his genuine invitations ("would love your take") stay |
| "Lead with the finding" | He leads with a reversal or a scene; the finding arrives second |
| "Anti-shape: one line per paragraph" | He uses short paragraphs with blank lines, and it reads as him |

The one generic rule that fully holds: **no evidence, no post.**
