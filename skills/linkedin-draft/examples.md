# Five real posts by Supreet

Published by him. Use them to hear the voice, not as text to reuse. Copying a sentence, an analogy,
or an opening from these is the same failure as inventing one.

Each example maps to one structure named in `SKILL.md`.

## 1. Setup → reversal

> The advice we got: "pharma moves too slow for a startup." The reality: 5 Top 10 pharma companies in 12 months.
>
> The narrative that enterprise pharma can't move fast is outdated. When the problem is real, and the ROI is clear, these organizations act, and act at scale.
>
> But the thing that changed most for Synthio Labs was this: we stopped trying to convince people AI was worth exploring and started talking to people who already knew it was, and just needed the right partner.
>
> Enterprise sales isn't about persuasion. It's about finding the right people who see the same future as you do.
>
> #FounderJourney #EnterpriseSales #GTM #Startups #SynthioLabs

**Why it works.** The first line does everything: a real quote he heard, then one number that
contradicts it. The reversal is not a rhetorical trick — he actually held the belief and the data
actually changed it.

**What to copy.** Two short clauses, contrast, then explain. Never manufacture the contrast; it has
to be something he was genuinely told or genuinely believed.

## 2. Outside idea → implication ("Weekend Pondering")

> 🧊 Back with my 'Weekend Pondering'
>
> I've been thinking about why enterprise AI feels so stuck, and honestly, the best explanation I've found comes from high school physics. Remember latent heat?
>
> Q = mL. The energy (Q) needed to change a substance's state equals its mass (m) times its latent heat (L). Nothing in that formula raises the temperature. Every joule goes into the transformation itself. So when you heat ice at 0°C, something weird happens. You keep adding energy, but the thermometer doesn't change. All that energy is busy turning ice into water. The state is changing; you just can't see it.
>
> That's enterprise AI right now. 🤖
>
> Companies are pouring billions in. Models, pilots, consultants, AI teams, copilots, data infrastructure, training programs. And executives keep asking the same question: "We're spending so much. Why isn't anything changing?"
>
> Here's the thing: the energy isn't wasted. It's being absorbed. It's going into changing workflows. Breaking down org boundaries. Rewriting incentives. Building trust. Navigating politics. Figuring out who gets to make decisions now. Convincing someone to hand over work they've owned for 20 years. And notice the m in the formula: the bigger the mass, the more energy the transformation takes. That's just physics.
>
> People call this inertia. I think that undersells it. Inertia says organizations just don't want to move. Latent heat says something more interesting: a massive amount of energy has to go in before you see any change at all. And then — almost suddenly — the whole state flips.
>
> That's my bet for enterprise AI. Adoption will look disappointingly slow for a while. Then organizations will cross the phase-transition point and will suddenly look like a completely different company.
>
> The mistake?
> 🌡️ Measuring the temperature while the ice is melting. Enjoy your weekend, and maybe watch some ice melt while you're at it. 🧊☀️

**Why it works.** He teaches the borrowed idea properly before using it. The formula is real, the
mechanism is explained, and only then does he map it. He also extends the mapping — `m` for company
size — which proves the analogy is load-bearing rather than decorative. He names his own bet and
what he thinks the mistake is.

**What to copy.** Explain the outside idea correctly first. If the mapping only works loosely, the
analogy is wrong and the post should not be written.

## 3. Scene → claim

> A year ago today, Sahitya walked into YC Alumni Demo Day in a white coat.
> Full physician attire. In front of 500+ founders and investors.
>
> Our point was: we know this world from the inside. We know what's broken. And we're fixing it with AI built specifically for this. For context, pharma spends $100B+ a year on commercial and GTM functions. Most of it is people, process, and software designed for a world that no longer exists.
>
> We're enabling the world's leading drug companies to show up for doctors and patients at their best, every single day. 5 of the top 10 global pharma companies are already building with us. The white coat had a point.
>
> To every team presenting at Y Combinator Demo Day this week: the conviction you bring to the stage matters. Good luck with your pitches and your raises.
>
> #YCombinator #Founders #DemoDay #PharmaAI #SynthioLabs #VoiceAI

**Why it works.** It opens on one thing that actually happened, with a specific detail he witnessed.
The claim arrives after the image. "The white coat had a point" closes the loop in six words.

**What to copy.** The scene must be a real moment he can date. A generic scene ("a founder asked me
last week") is the anti-pattern this shape is most often faked with.

## 4. Two-column judgment

> 🥁🥁🥁 SaaS is down 30%.... and everyone's panicking. I wrote something about why the stock drop is actually the least interesting part of this story. The real question is which vendors will cannibalize themselves before someone else does it for them.
>
> The CIO sniff test is simple:
> ❗They're selling you the same platform you've had for a decade, while offering AI as a free feature bolted on top, a chatbot here, a "smart" dashboard there. They are too big to actually pivot. This is the Law of Inertia at institutional scale. They are not transforming; they are preserving margin while they figure out how to delay the inevitable.
>
> ✅ They come to you and say: "Everything we sold you for the past decade is now deprecated, effectively zero value." Then they show you something genuinely different. Not the same wine in a new bottle. A fundamentally new architecture.
>
> Almost no one does the second thing voluntarily. The ones who do tend to have a founder's conviction or nothing left to lose.
>
> Full piece linked below. Would love your take if you're in the room for these vendor conversations.
>
> #Pharma #AI #SaaS #EnterpriseAI #PharmaCommercial #SynthioLabs

**Why it works.** He hands the reader a test they can run on Monday. He criticises a pattern, never
a named competitor. The closing invitation is specific — "if you're in the room for these vendor
conversations" — which is why it does not read as engagement bait.

**What to copy.** ❗ and ✅ as the two columns. Make the test usable by the reader; if it only
describes a problem, it is not this shape.

## 5. Micro

> We talk about experience wrong in AI. Mental models built before 2022 often pull people in the wrong direction.
>
> Actively trying to unlearn and relearn every day.

**Why it works.** One thought, no build-up, no hashtags, no emoji. He admits he is still working on
it rather than instructing anyone.

**What to copy.** When a thought is complete in three lines, stop. Padding it into an essay is the
same failure as filling a calendar slot.

## Milestones

Product and company news is his most frequent type but has no special shape: state the news, give
the concrete capability or number, add a short invitation. It reliably earns less engagement than
the five shapes above, so when two ideas are equally ready, draft the personal one.
